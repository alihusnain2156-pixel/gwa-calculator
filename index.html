How to Compute Your GWA in the Philippines

Your General Weighted Average (GWA) is the single most important number on your transcript. It decides whether you qualify for Latin honors, keep your scholarship, get into graduate school, or meet an employer's cut-off. Computing it is simple once you understand that it is a weighted average — subjects with more units count more.

Step by step

  • Step 1 — List your subjects. Write down each subject, its final grade, and the number of credit units.
  • Step 2 — Multiply grade by units. For every subject, multiply the final grade by its units. This gives the "weighted grade points."
  • Step 3 — Add everything up. Sum all the weighted grade points, and separately sum all the units.
  • Step 4 — Divide. Divide the total weighted grade points by the total units. That is your GWA.

The calculator above does all of this instantly — but knowing the manual method helps you double-check and understand why a heavy major subject affects your average more than a 1-unit elective.

The GWA Formula

GWA = Σ (Grade × Units) ÷ Σ (Total Units)

In words: multiply each subject's grade by its units, add all those products together, then divide by the total number of units you carried that term.

GWA = 24.25 ÷ 15 = 1.62 — which falls in the Cum Laude range at most Philippine universities.

Philippine Grade Scale (1.00–5.00)

The Philippine college scale is inverted compared to the US GPA scale: a lower number is better. A 1.00 is a perfect grade, while a 5.00 is failing. This is the opposite of the American 4.0 system, where 4.0 is best.

Latin Honors & Dean's List GWA Requirements

Latin honors are awarded at graduation based on your cumulative GWA across your entire degree, while the Dean's List is awarded per semester. Most schools also require no failing grades, no unresolved INC, and no major disciplinary record.

GWA by University

Most Philippine universities — including UP, PUP, UST, FEU, NU and Mapúa — use the standard 1.00–5.00 scale, so the same formula applies. A few important exceptions:

  • UP (University of the Philippines): uses 4.00 as a conditional pass and DRP for dropped subjects (excluded from GWA). Recognition is University Scholar / College Scholar, not Dean's List.
  • DLSU (De La Salle University): uses a 4.0 ascending scale where 4.0 is the highest. A generic 1.00–5.00 calculator will give the wrong result — select DLSU above to use the correct scale.
  • Ateneo (ADMU): uses letter grades (A, B+, B, C+, C, F) that map to a 4.0 quality-point system.
  • UST (University of Santo Tomas): grades internally in percentage but reports on the 1.00–5.00 scale.
  • Mapúa & TIP: use QPI (Quality Point Index) on a 0–4 ascending scale.
  • Senior High School (DepEd): uses a percentage system where 75% is passing — completely different from college.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do you compute GWA in the Philippines?
Multiply each subject's final grade by its credit units, add all the products together, then divide by the total number of units. The formula is GWA = Σ(Grade × Units) ÷ Σ(Units).
What GWA is needed for Cum Laude?
At most Philippine universities Cum Laude requires a cumulative GWA of 1.46–1.75, Magna Cum Laude 1.21–1.45, and Summa Cum Laude 1.00–1.20 — all with zero failing grades anywhere in your record.
Is a 1.75 GWA good?
Yes. A 1.75 GWA (around 87–88%) is the standard Cum Laude and Dean's List threshold and qualifies for most scholarships such as DOST-SEI and CHED.
What is the passing GWA in college?
A grade of 3.00 is the minimum passing grade on the 1.00–5.00 scale. A 4.00 is a conditional pass (requires a removal exam) and a 5.00 is failing.
Is GWA the same as GPA?
No. GWA uses the inverted 1.00–5.00 Philippine scale (1.00 is best). GPA uses the 4.0 US scale (4.0 is best). This calculator converts between them automatically.
Can senior high school students use this?
Yes — choose "Senior High School (DepEd)" in the school dropdown to compute using the percentage-based DepEd system instead of the college 1.00–5.00 scale.
How do I compute my cumulative GWA?
You cannot simply average your semester GWAs. Use the "Cumulative GWA" tab and enter every subject (or every semester's totals) with its units, because longer/heavier semesters carry more weight.
